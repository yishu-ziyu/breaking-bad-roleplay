# kid-30-second — Breaking Bad Roleplay Landing

**Lens**: opened on a phone, one thumb, 30 seconds, no reading
**Subject**: bb.yishuziyu.cn landing (current target, 1440 capture)
**Date**: 2026-07-12

The page is technically there. It is also forgettable in a way that costs the project its first 5 seconds.

---

- **The hero title block wins the eye in 5 seconds.** "BREAKING BAD / WORLD LINES" plus the dark plate with a thin yellow rule is the only thing a thumb notices. Problem: it sits on the left, the human figure sits on the right, and the two never meet. The eye lands on the title, drifts right, hits the man, and there is no connective tissue between them. The world feels split in half. C2-Detroit solves this by overlapping title and face; C1-Disco lets the painting swallow the text. BB does neither and the composition reads like a slide deck.
  - **Missing**: a single visible moment where Walter is *talking*, not standing. His mouth is closed, his hands are empty, his pose is mid-stride to nowhere. A frozen mouth mid-line, or a held cigarette, or a notebook in his hand would turn the right half from "stock photo of a man in a hazmat suit" into "someone I could talk to."
  - **Smallest fix**: replace the still photo with a portrait crop where Walter is mid-gesture and his eyes look slightly off-camera (toward the user). Tighten the crop so his face occupies the top-right quadrant, not the middle distance.
  - **Reference**: C2-Detroit. One eye, one face, no apology.

- **The three-step pill row (1 选择 > 2 锚定 > 3 对话) is the weakest part of the page.** It reads like a SaaS onboarding tooltip that escaped its product. "选择 锚定 对话" are abstract verbs that tell a stranger nothing about who they will meet or what they will do. By 10 seconds the user still does not know whether this is a quiz, a chatbot, a game, or a story.
  - **Missing**: the word "Walter", "Jesse", or "Gus" anywhere in the first viewport. The page is about specific people and none of them is named.
  - **Smallest fix**: swap the three pills for three named character chips — "Walter White", "Jesse Pinkman", "Saul Goodman" — each with a tiny face thumbnail. The verb becomes the face.
  - **Reference**: C1-Disco names the protagonist on the page. C2-Detroit names Connor on the page. BB names nobody on the page.

- **"进入世界" is the only CTA and it is positioned wrong.** It sits under the step pills, which means the eye has already passed it by the time it registers the title. The button is the same dark amber as the title accent, so it disappears into the same color family. The strongest button on the page should be the brightest object on the page.
  - **Missing**: a single high-contrast primary action that visibly says "click here to begin with Walter." Right now the page offers "Enter the World" which is a vibe, not an instruction.
  - **Smallest fix**: change CTA label to "和 Walter 聊聊" and increase its size so it reads as the page's anchor, not its caption.
  - **Reference**: C1-Disco — its gold button is small but it sits at the bottom edge like a deliberate relic, not a sub-element.

- **The atmosphere is correct but underfed.** The street, the police lights, the RV silhouette in the haze, the sodium yellow — these are right. The problem is that this environment reads as a screenshot, not as a place. C1-Disco's painterly chaos fills two-thirds of the viewport with brushstrokes you can almost feel. BB's background is one frozen photo at 30% opacity behind a black plate. It is more wallpaper than world.
  - **Missing**: a visible second layer of atmosphere — rain streaks, smoke, neon bleed, or a slow-moving image — that signals the page is alive. A static hero says "this is a poster." A breathing hero says "this is a place."
  - **Smallest fix**: add a slow parallax Ken-Burns drift on the background image (0.3x speed) and a subtle vignette pulse around the title plate. Two CSS animations. No new assets.
  - **Reference**: C1-Disco painterly chaos, C2-Detroit eye-mist hybrid.

- **The yellow accent system is doing too much work.** Title, CTA, border rule, step numbers, underline — all in the same amber. The user has no color hierarchy to follow. C2-Detroit uses cool blue + stark white to separate product from chrome. C1-Disco uses two muted metals (gold and silver) for two distinct CTAs. BB uses one color for five jobs.
  - **Missing**: a second accent color that separates the *invitation* (title + atmosphere) from the *action* (CTA + character chips).
  - **Smallest fix**: keep amber for the world (title, rule, atmosphere). Introduce a single near-white (#F5F0E6) for the CTA fill and the chip faces. The CTA becomes the only bright thing on the page.
  - **Reference**: C2-Detroit's white-on-blue split.

- **The page does not survive the 15-second test.** At 15 seconds the user has scanned everything once and is looking for a reason to stay or scroll. There is no visible character line of dialogue, no quote, no moment that earns a second look. The subtitle "走进阿尔伯克基。你的每一句话，他们都会记住。" is doing this work alone and the typography is too small and too grey to compete with the title.
  - **Missing**: one short line of dialogue from a character — five words in Walter's voice, set in a larger serif, sitting between the subtitle and the CTA. Example: "*I am the one who knocks.*" Even a stranger to the show recognizes the cadence.
  - **Smallest fix**: pull a five-word line from one character and render it in a 32px serif above the CTA, italicized. The page stops being a homepage and starts being a scene.
  - **Reference**: C1-Disco — its body copy reads like prose, not product copy.

- **At 30 seconds the user leaves because nothing moved.** A static, well-composed page still feels like a brochure. The thumb stops because there is no signal that something will happen on click — no preview, no character voice, no animated cursor, no visible second screen below. The page has no below-the-fold invitation in this capture either: it is one viewport and then darkness.
  - **Missing**: a visible hint of what happens after the click — a faint preview tile of a conversation, a typed-character animation, or a single quote that previews Walter's first reply.
  - **Smallest fix**: add a 48px second viewport strip below the hero with one card: "Walter: 你来干什么。" — styled like a chat bubble in muted type. The user now knows what they will see if they click.
  - **Reference**: C2-Detroit screenshot grid (six stills visible) — the page shows you what you will get.

## Out-of-scope drift signals

These bullets point at things that would need a content or marketing call, not a UI fix:

- Which character to feature first is a product decision (Walter vs. Jesse vs. Gus as the lead).
- Whether the subtitle stays in Chinese or shifts to English affects who the page is for.
- Whether "Breaking Bad" as a brand-mark can be used on a non-official product is a legal/IP question, not a taste question.
