# Studio Buyer Diagnostic — bb.yishuziyu.cn (2026-07-12)

**Lens**: commercial seriousness — would a licensing buyer at an HBO-tier studio greenlight this for a first meeting.

**Verdict in one line**: the page has atmosphere but no IP-stewardship vocabulary; the title and the headline read as fan fiction, and the page never earns the right to call itself "World Lines."

---

## Defects

- **The "BREAKING BAD / WORLD LINES" title lockup sits inside a dark glass card, separated from the character image.**
  *Commercial signal failed*: the title is the most literal possible naming of the source IP, set in the same neutral grotesque that any blog would use. A licensing buyer reads this as someone who owns a fan project, not a partner.
  *Smallest fix*: drop the "World Lines" subtitle and replace the first line with the show's iconic element symbol or a hand-set Roman numeral. Move the title out of the card and into the negative space over the road. **Reference**: C1 Disco — the wordmark is the illustration, not a label above it.

- **No character close-up dominates the frame.**
  *Commercial signal failed*: the only human is mid-distance, backlit, small in frame. There is no face a buyer can cast against. C1 and C2 both commit to a single figure you cannot look away from; BB treats its protagonist like scenery.
  *Smallest fix*: tighten the crop on the standing figure to a 3/4 shot of the face, lifted into the right half of the viewport. The character should be the page, not its satellite. **Reference**: C2 Detroit — one eye, no apology.

- **The three-step indicator ("选择 / 锚定 / 对话") looks like a form wizard, not a story hook.**
  *Commercial signal failed*: numbered orange bullets in dark pills is the visual vocabulary of a SaaS onboarding funnel. A studio buyer reads this as "you have built a chatbot on top of our IP."
  *Smallest fix*: replace the three pills with one sentence in the same serif voice as the title, set in italic, no numbers, no icons. Or remove entirely and let the CTA do the work. **Reference**: C1 Disco has no steps, only atmosphere and a single buy button.

- **The primary CTA is a flat gold-outline rectangle labeled "进入世界".**
  *Commercial signal failed*: the button is the most generic interactive element on the page. There is no ceremonial weight to entering. A buyer expects entry to feel earned, not clicked.
  *Smallest fix*: set the CTA in the same display face as the title, give it a small chevron and a subtitle line (e.g., "Begin with Walter White") instead of a generic verb. Sit it on the bottom edge of the viewport, not inside a card. **Reference**: C1 Disco and C2 Detroit both place their primary action at the corner with restraint, almost whispering.

- **There is no evidence of craft anywhere below the fold-level signals visible.**
  *Commercial signal failed*: the page shows what the product is, not that someone made it well. No award row, no press quotes, no captioned screenshot grid, no "AVAILABLE ON" beat. C1 and C2 both use their lower half to prove the property is taken seriously by someone other than its makers.
  *Smallest fix*: add a single film-still row beneath the hero, captioned in small caps, plus a quiet credit line ("set photography, dialogue models, three narrative modes"). One row is enough. **Reference**: C2 Detroit — six captioned screenshots plus an "AVAILABLE ON" rail.

- **The yellow accent color is used functionally, not atmospherically.**
  *Commercial signal failed*: yellow is doing the work of a brand color (hover states, step bullets, CTA border) rather than reading as the sodium-vapor street light the world is set in. C1 lets its palette bleed across the whole image. C2 commits to a single cool blue and holds it.
  *Smallest fix*: lift the yellow into the photograph itself — add a sodium-vapor tint pass across the asphalt and the figure — and remove yellow from every UI element except the CTA hover. The page should look warm because of the image, not because of the CSS. **Reference**: C1 Disco — the warm tones live in the illustration; the UI chrome is restrained.

- **Typography hierarchy has exactly two voices: a display sans and a Chinese body face, both at default weights.**
  *Commercial signal failed*: there is no editorial voice. A buyer expects display, subhead, body, caption with distinct weights and cases. The current page reads as a single template rendered once.
  *Smallest fix*: introduce a contrasting italic serif for the headline subtitle (the "走进阿尔伯克基" line) and a tracked-out small-caps caption under it. One additional font weight will do more than another visual. **Reference**: C3 Cyberpunk — even the maximalist reference sets its title in a custom industrial face with letterform intent, not a system font.

## Out-of-scope drift signals

These notes require IP counsel, not design work, and should not appear in the design backlog: the literal string "BREAKING BAD" as page title would not survive a Sony/IP clearance review and must be replaced by a device mark; the standing figure is recognizably in costume and would need either license-holder-supplied stills or a wholly original character before studio presentation.
